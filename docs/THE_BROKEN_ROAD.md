# The Broken Road

## Setting

The borderlands lie between prosperous beacon cities and scattered frontier settlements. Humans, elves, dwarves, and orcs share roads, markets, and companies; allegiance and personality matter more than ancestry.

Beacon cities maintain magical instruction, reagent markets, and temples. Frontier villages rarely have a resident spellcaster. A company can bring magic into those places, but must carry the supplies and pay the wages that sustain it.

A spell slot represents the focus a caster can safely bind before needing deliberate recovery. Food, herbs, time, and city services turn magical power into a company-management choice. A wizard who spends every slot on an early ambush leaves the evening journey exposed. These are original spells and rules inspired by familiar slot-based fantasy systems.

## Playable chapter: Hungry Roads

Greyhaven's food shortage draws the company into a disputed grain shipment. Ashbrook's abandoned farm, Merrit's interrupted trade route, and Captain Venn's raiders give the shortage visible causes. Investigating the farm, restoring the escort route, and defeating the stronghold open a choice over the captured grain.

The League rewards the return of its shipment. The villagers benefit from restored farmland. Keeping the grain sustains the company at the cost of crime and continuing shortages. Companion investigations and recurring travelers carry personal consequences alongside the regional decision.

## Future campaign direction — not yet implemented

The next chapters can follow Venn's invoice into Iron Hollow, connect scarce frontier reagents to beacon-city interests, and reveal who profits from broken roads. Branches should alter local livelihoods, faction trust, and access to magical services rather than only changing dialogue.

The eventual campaign should give the company a choice between protecting the city-controlled beacon network, helping frontier communities build independent access, or exploiting the trade itself. Magic remains high fantasy while its availability, spell slots, and recovery costs keep travel and camp decisions meaningful.
